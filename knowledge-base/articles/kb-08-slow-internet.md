# Slow Internet: Common Causes and What to Check

**Article:** KB-08 · **Category:** Internet Connection & Speed · **Applies to:** BlazzRouter R100, R200

If your internet feels slower than usual, the cause is usually something inside your home, not the Blazz network. Here's how to find out.

## Step 1: Check the router light

Look at your BlazzRouter. If the light isn't solid green, start with "What the Lights on Your BlazzRouter and BlazzBox Mean." If it's solid green, keep going.

## Step 2: Run a speed test the right way

Speed tests over Wi-Fi are almost always lower than your plan speed, so they can be misleading. For an accurate result:

1. Plug a computer into one of the router's Ethernet ports with a cable.
2. Close other apps, and pause big downloads or streaming on other devices.
3. Run a speed test in your web browser.

## Step 3: Compare the result to your plan speed

Your plan speed is shown on your bill.

- **80% of your plan speed or more:** Your connection is healthy. If Wi-Fi still feels slow, the cause is your Wi-Fi, not the line. See the causes below.
- **Between 50% and 80%:** This can happen during busy evening hours, from 7 p.m. to 11 p.m. See "Is Slower Internet in the Evening Normal?" If you see this outside those hours on several tests, restart your router once. If it doesn't improve, contact Blazz Support.
- **Below 50%, on more than one test:** Restart your router once. If it's still below 50% after the restart, contact Blazz Support.

## Common causes of slow Wi-Fi

- **Distance and walls.** The farther you are from the router, and the more walls in between, the slower Wi-Fi gets. See "Wi-Fi Keeps Cutting In and Out: Check Where Your Router Is."
- **Too many devices at once.** Everyone streaming, gaming, or downloading at the same time shares one connection.
- **The wrong Wi-Fi network.** On a BlazzRouter R100, the network ending in "-5G" is faster up close, and the other network reaches farther but is slower. The BlazzRouter R200 picks the best option for you automatically.
- **An older device.** Older phones and laptops may not support faster Wi-Fi.
- **A large home or several floors.** See "Weak Wi-Fi in Some Rooms: BlazzMesh Extenders."

## Quick things to try

- Move closer to the router and test again.
- Restart the device that feels slow.
- Use an Ethernet cable for devices that don't move, like a desktop computer or game console.
