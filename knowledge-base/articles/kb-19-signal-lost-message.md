# "Signal Lost" Message on Your BlazzBox: What It Means

**Article:** KB-19 · **Category:** BlazzBox & TV · **Applies to:** BlazzBox X150, X200

If your TV shows a Blazz screen with the message **"Signal Lost,"** your BlazzBox is on and connected to your TV, but it can't reach the Blazz TV service. Your BlazzBox gets TV through your BlazzRouter, so the problem is usually the router, or the connection between the box and the router.

## "Signal Lost" vs. "No Signal"

- **"Signal Lost" on a Blazz screen** comes from your BlazzBox. Follow the steps below.
- **"No Signal" from your TV itself** (often a small box that moves around the screen) means your TV isn't getting anything from the BlazzBox through the HDMI cable. See "HDMI Problems Between Your BlazzBox and TV."

## What to do

1. **Check the router light.** If it's not solid green, your internet is down too. Fix the router first — see "What the Lights on Your BlazzRouter and BlazzBox Mean." Once the router is solid green, TV usually comes back on its own within a few minutes.
2. **Check the BlazzBox light.** If it's blinking amber, the box is trying to connect — wait 10 minutes. If it's still blinking amber after that, or it's solid red, keep going.
3. **Check the connection between the box and the router.**
   - **BlazzBox X150:** It always uses an Ethernet cable. Make sure the cable is firmly plugged in at both the box and the router.
   - **BlazzBox X200 with a cable:** Same as above.
   - **BlazzBox X200 on Wi-Fi:** The box may be too far from the router. If your Wi-Fi password was changed recently, the box needs the new one. On your TV, go to Settings, then Network, then Wi-Fi.
4. **Restart the BlazzBox once.** See "How to Restart Your BlazzBox."
5. If the router is solid green but the box light is still solid red after the restart — or still blinking amber 30 minutes after it — contact Blazz Support to book a technician visit.

## Tip for the BlazzBox X200

If you often see "Signal Lost" while the box is on Wi-Fi, connect it to the router with an Ethernet cable instead. It's much more stable. See "Why a Wired Connection Is More Stable Than Wi-Fi."
